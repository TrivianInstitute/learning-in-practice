# Fictional worked example: a claim ledger session

**FICTIONAL TRAINING EXAMPLE — NO SESSION OCCURRED.** Names, events, and observations below are invented to show how to write a bounded field note. Do not cite this as evidence of learning or a pilot.

**Resource:** [Interaction Literacy claim ledger](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interaction-literacy/blob/main/exercises/claim-ledger.md)  
**Setting:** invented adult community workshop  
**Time:** about 30 minutes  
**Question:** What makes an AI-generated claim suitable for a public notice?

## Invented observation

A facilitator presented a fictional AI answer claiming a local service would change its opening hours. Participants worked in pairs from a printed handout. In the imagined session, several pairs initially wrote “check online” without specifying a source; after a prompt asking who could authoritatively confirm the hours, the pairs named the service's official notice or direct contact.

## Interpretation and alternative

The facilitator might infer that asking for an authoritative source made the checking step more specific. Another explanation is that the second prompt simply clarified the task; the original exercise instructions may have been vague. Nothing here would establish transfer to a new task, durable learning, or a causal effect.

## Unknown and change

We do not know how learners would act outside this invented classroom or whether different access needs were met. A proposed revision is to add a “source and authority” box to the handout, then invite facilitators to report whether learners find the instruction clear.
