# How to contribute a field note

A field note is a carefully bounded account of teaching and learning, shared to improve a resource. It is not a testimonial, evaluation result, or research dataset.

## 1. Decide whether a public report is appropriate

Before drafting, read [Privacy and publication](publication/privacy-and-consent.md). Ask: Did the setting permit a public account? Could the combination of location, date, group size, learner characteristics, or incidents identify someone? Did participants know that learning from a session might be shared? A participant's agreement to attend is not agreement to publish an account.

If permission or de-identification is uncertain, **do not create a public issue or PR**. Contact learn@trivianinstitute.org for a private process or send only a general resource suggestion with no field narrative.

## 2. Draft locally

Copy the [field note template](templates/field-note.md) into a private working document. Include a broad setting and approximate age range only if safe, the source resource and any adaptations, what you directly observed, your interpretation, an alternative explanation, and one proposed revision. Remove quotations from learners and raw artifacts. For youth settings, use an authorized adult reporting route and aggregate observations where appropriate.

## 3. Review before sharing

Use the [submission guide](SUBMISSION_GUIDE.md). Read the note as if a participant, caregiver, colleague, or local community member might see it. If any detail could point to them, generalize or omit it. Confirm that you are authorized to publish and license your contribution. When in doubt, do not submit a public note.

## 4. Submit and revise

An eligible note can be proposed as a PR under reports/ using the template. Maintainers may request a narrower account, further permissions, or a private discussion; they may decline publication. Accepted notes retain their author and context, and later corrections should be attached rather than silently replacing inconvenient observations.

## 5. Synthesize cautiously

Compare reviewed notes with [claim levels](methods/claim-levels.md). Use the [learning review template](templates/learning-review.md) to identify recurring questions and resource revisions. Do not infer causality or general effectiveness from anecdotal accounts.

The [fictional example](examples/fictional-field-note.md) illustrates the form without involving real learners.
